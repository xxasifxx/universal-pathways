# Plain-language copy audit

## Goal
Rewrite the public site and review room in direct, ordinary English. Remove the artificial contrasts, policy jargon, dramatic framing, and overexplaining that keep making the copy sound written by a consultant.

## Changes
- Audit all visitor-facing copy on the home, priorities, volunteer, header, footer, prompts, and forms.
- Audit all review-room copy, including priorities research, budget explanations, PILOT material, township-growth drafts, review instructions, and notes UI.
- Replace phrases such as “not only,” “rather than,” “lever,” “mechanism,” “on the strength of,” “what sits behind,” and “plain English” when they create a forced or self-conscious voice.
- Rewrite dense passages into shorter factual sentences without changing figures, dates, source claims, policy meaning, or the candidate’s first-person biography.
- Keep formal terms only where they are the real name of a law, filing, policy, accounting line, or government process.
- Make the public priorities especially short: plain promise statements with no research argument added back in.

## Copy standard
- Say the point once.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
- Avoid “not X, but Y” and “not only X” constructions.
- Avoid telling readers that wording is clear, careful, honest, useful, or plain.
- Avoid campaign slogans unless they are part of the approved campaign material.
- Separate documented facts from open questions without defensive language.

## Verification
- Search the edited files again for the flagged patterns and known banned claims.
- Check the public pages and each review tab in the browser at mobile and desktop widths.
- Confirm the current build is clean.
