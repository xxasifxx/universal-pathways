## 2. Make the dashboard actually say something

Right now the page shows one donut and stops. The PDF carries three years side by side — 2024-25 actual, 2025-26 revised, 2026-27 proposed — which is where the real story is. Add three sections below the donut.

**Where the money comes from.** A revenue panel, because a spending chart alone hides the part residents feel:
- Local tax levy $159,811,059, which is 76.4% of the general fund
- State aid $41,368,043, 19.8%
- Federal Medicaid reimbursement $103,844, plus $2,322,500 of fund balance and $2,537,290 transferred in
- Levy up 6.5% in one year and 10.2% over two, while total state aid is down 1.3% over the same two years. Equalization aid fell from $26.5M to $19.0M and has only partly recovered to $23.3M.

**What is growing and what is not.** A ranked movement list, two-year change from 2024-25 actual to 2026-27 proposed:
- Out-of-district tuition +54.5% ($4.28M -> $6.61M)
- Charter school transfer +26.7% ($5.89M -> $7.46M)
- Employee benefits +12.5% ($35.9M -> $40.4M), now 38.43% of salaries, up from 30.16% two years ago
- Transportation +8.8%
- Operations and maintenance +3.6%
- Regular programs instruction +0.2% ($49.54M -> $49.63M), essentially flat
- Capital outlay -28.3% ($11.79M -> $8.45M)

That contrast is the whole argument the site has been making in prose, and here it is in the district's own filing: the levy rose 10.2% while classroom instruction stayed flat, because the growth went to benefits, out-of-district placements, and charter transfers.

**What is left in the bank.** The Recapitulation of Balances page, plainly:
- Unrestricted general operating fund balance $7,982,530 (6/30/2024) -> $7,098,724 -> est. $6,314,555 -> est. $3,992,055 by 6/30/2027
- Capital reserve $3,093,267 -> est. $256,697
- Legal reserve $4,084,826 -> $0
A district spending down reserves is buying time, and the FY2028 budget is where that runs out. This is stated as what the numbers show, not as a prediction.

**Per pupil, in context.** Total budgetary comparative per pupil cost $20,731 proposed, against $19,096 two years ago. Classroom instruction is $11,882 of it; administration $1,929. Enrollment 8,559 on roll, up from 8,393.

**Tax rate.** Estimated total school tax rate 8.2709 per $100 of net taxable valuation, off a $1,934,304,750 net taxable base; total school levy including debt service $159,983,631.

Each of these is a labeled figure with its source line named, in the same restrained voice as the rest of the site. No exclamation, no "shocking," no three-beat closers.

## 4. Rewrite the PILOT page against verified sources

The current page rests on a Facebook comment. Research turned up real reporting, and one thing that does not hold up.

**Remove:** the "$1.2 million in reported PILOT revenue held in municipal reserve" badge in the hero. No published source states that figure. The Redevelopment Agency's FY2025 authority budget lists reserve categories but no retrievable figure matching it. Keeping an unverifiable number in the largest type on the page is the fastest way to lose the argument.

**Replace it with the split, which is documented.** From TAPinto's Jan 30, 2026 explainer: ordinary property taxes run roughly 65% schools, 15% county, 20% township, while under a PILOT roughly 95% goes to the township, about 5% to the county, and none directly to the schools. The land underneath keeps being taxed normally; it is the improvements that are converted to a negotiated payment. That is the mechanism, in the township's own framing.

**Add what the record shows has happened here:**
- Brightview Senior Living, Cranbury Road and Ryders Lane: the Redevelopment Agency recommended a PILOT and financial agreement on Aug 5, 2024 (Resolution 2024013) and reaffirmed it Oct 21, 2024 (Resolution 2024017). The minutes record the recommendation but no dollar amount or term, which is itself worth saying.
- Warnsdorfer Elementary: Township Administrator Joseph Criscuolo and Mayor Brad Cohen have both said PILOT money paid for the parking lot repaving, and Cohen has said a snack bar and outdoor bathrooms at the high school football field are planned for 2026 or 2027 (Patch, Feb 25, 2026).
- Criscuolo, at Council: "The board of ed still gets the land value on those agreements," and "The school system gets all the money they ask for truly" (eyesoneb.com, May 15, 2026). Quote both directly and let them stand.
- State law limits PILOT money directed at schools to capital work; it cannot go into the operating budget. Pending bill S-1807 would require sharing PILOT revenue with districts. The BOE received a legislative update on exactly this (TAPinto, Feb 13, 2026).

**And say what is not on the record.** The actual annual payment amounts and terms for the named agreements are not available in any accessible public document; the ordinances are posted as scans or return errors. That is a specific, checkable ask a board member can make in public, and the page will say so instead of filling the gap with a guess.
