# Coherence repair pass

## Goal
Fix sentences that became awkward, vague, or logically disconnected during the plain-language rewrite. Preserve the candidate's meaning and every sourced fact.

## Confirmed problems

### Bullets that describe process instead of a promise
Two public bullets under Students First lost their point:
- "Use more than one measure to place students in advanced courses" describes an internal method. A parent cannot tell what changes for their child.
- "Allow placement exams, including languages" sounds like paperwork rather than the real promise: a student who already knows the material can prove it and move up.

Rewrite both as the outcome for the student:
- "A student ready for an advanced course can take it, even if one grade says otherwise."
- "A student who already knows the material can test into the higher course, including in world languages."

Then check the remaining bullets under all three priorities for the same failure and rewrite any that name a procedure instead of a result.

### Other broken lines
- "Universally paid public full-day Pre-K" is malformed. Use "Free full-day Pre-K for every family."
- "Grade students on their work, not their homes" is an unclear metaphor. State it directly: less of a grade riding on homework, more on work done in class.
- "Replace buildings when repeated repairs cost more" is missing the comparison. Name it: repair costs versus replacement.
- "Use state construction grants to build for lower maintenance" merges funding and goal into one confusing line. Split them.
- Compressed review-room fragments, including "what a bridge program adds on top of existing bilingual staffing" and truncated "Still unknown" entries.
- Headings and transitions that no longer match the text beneath them.


## Method
- Read each sentence with the sentence before and after it; edit the whole passage when a one-line substitution would break the logic.
- Keep the public priorities brief, but use complete statements that a voter can understand without background knowledge.
- Keep formal policy and budget terms in the review room, followed by a direct explanation when needed.
- Do not introduce new arguments, promises, figures, or political claims.
- Do not force every sentence into the same short cadence. Use enough connective language to preserve cause and effect.

## Verification
- Review every changed passage in its rendered section, not as an isolated string.
- Check public and review pages for fragments, unclear pronouns, unsupported transitions, and contradictions between summaries and details.
- Confirm the site builds cleanly and that mobile layouts still fit.
